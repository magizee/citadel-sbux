# Anticipated pushback (and honest answers)

Have these ready. Each item is backed by work in `../_research_archive/`.

| Pushback | Honest answer |
|---|---|
| **"Comps are +7.9% with transactions +4.2% (Q3 FY26). The turnaround is working."** | Comps are real for surviving stores. The point is that comps exclude closed stores: ~1,100 locations closed since Oct 2025 (incl. licensed), and ~85–90% of their visits were not recaptured nearby. System-wide traffic is weaker than comps imply. |
| **"Starbucks is holding share vs Dunkin' nationally."** | **True, and it is the strongest counter-evidence.** Starbucks' share of Starbucks + Dunkin' visits was flat YoY (78.11% → 78.13%, Jun–Sep), and Starbucks same-store visits grew +2.65% vs Dunkin' +1.02%, with 3.3% fewer locations. Answer: the bear case is local and forward-looking (closure-heavy metros lost relative ground; manager-vacancy metros slightly weaker, ρ −0.20), not a national share loss. Charts `12`, `13`. |
| **"Isn't transfer from closures inflating comps?"** | Only slightly. A before/after model finds +0.6% (not significant) for stores < 1 km and +1.8% at 1–2 km, with ≈ 4–12% recapture. Don't overclaim transfer; the stronger point is demand **lost**. |
| **"Low recapture just means customers went to other Starbucks further away."** | Possible for some; recapture is measured within 5 km and is a lower bound. But closed stores were low-traffic and shared fewer customers with neighbours, so a large share likely left the system. Check total-system visits to confirm. |
| **"Closing weak stores is good for margins."** | Probably yes for margins. The bear point is about the *growth narrative*: closures remove traffic that comps never show, and unit growth is slowing. Be clear this is a durability / valuation argument. |
| **"The closures were announced; this is priced in."** | Likely partly. The new information is the **spring 2026 wave (~300)** and the measured **low recapture** (~4–12%): closed stores' customers mostly did not move to nearby Starbucks. Check consensus expectations for comps after the lap. |
| **"Isn't Starbucks struggling to hire?"** | **No, and don't claim it.** Frontline hiring is a standing pipeline at 99% of stores; requisitions created in 2026 are ~17–20% *below* 2025 (consistent with lower turnover); store-manager share of postings is down vs 2025. Only *manager* vacancies show persistent pockets. |
| **"Are stores understaffed?" (if you cite employee surveys)** | Use the surveys as the labor evidence; the job data **cannot** measure headcount. What the job data shows: every store carries the same ~1.9 open requisitions whether it gets 1,100 or 4,300 visits a week (recruiting is not scaled to demand), and the busiest stores are slightly more likely to have duplicate frontline requisitions (2.4% vs 1.1%). Frontline requisitions overall are ~17–20% *below* 2025. Prefer 2026 surveys (after the 2025 labor investment) and pair union surveys with a neutral source. |
| **"Do manager vacancies hurt traffic?"** | Not detectable. Once closure transfer is removed, stores near manager vacancies show no traffic difference. Present vacancies as an execution *risk* management itself flags, not a demonstrated cause. |
| **"Isn't Advan GPS data unreliable in dense cities?"** | Visit *levels* in dense urban areas may be understated, so treat the 2:1 per-store gap as indicative. The before/after model compares each store with itself and with same-metro controls over the same weeks, so it is much less exposed. |
| **"Advan includes licensed stores."** | Yes. Closure counts include licensed locations. The foot-traffic tests use only stores matched to company-operated locations (9,419 of 9,979). |
| **"Openings are 653 → 510 → 135, so unit growth is collapsing?"** | Don't lean on 2026: Advan adds new locations with a lag, so recent openings are undercounted. Use company-reported net new stores instead. |
| **"How do you know the job data is complete?"** | 19,831 postings = 100.8% of Starbucks' own U.S. count; 395 searches, 0 incomplete; QA report in the archive. |
| **"Isn't this one quarter of foot traffic?"** | Yes: one 13-week window vs the prior year. The monitoring plan (re-run monthly) addresses this. |
